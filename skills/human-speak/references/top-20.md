# The 20 strongest AI writing giveaways

This is an editorial priority list for reviewing public writing. The order reflects recognizability and Nate Herk's editing preferences, not measured detection accuracy. People use these patterns too. Judge the passage in context and preserve the writer's meaning and voice.

Use [patterns.md](patterns.md) for the full rules and exceptions, then [evaluation.md](evaluation.md) to check the result.

1. **Formulaic contrasts.** "It's not X, it's Y." "You don't have a tools problem. You have a systems problem." State the claim directly unless the contrast corrects a real misunderstanding.
2. **Announcing the next point.** "Here's the kicker." "Here's where it gets interesting." Delete the announcement and make the point.
3. **Negative fragments.** "No coding. No complexity. Just results." "No setup, no cost." Write a complete factual sentence, including when the individual claims are concrete.
4. **Secret-insight framing.** "Nobody talks about this." "What most people miss." State and support the insight without inventing universal ignorance.
5. **Fake question-and-answer splits.** "The biggest problem? Trust." "The result? A transformation." Join the thought into a declarative sentence. Keep actual questions.
6. **Reaction instructions.** "Let that sink in." "Read that again." Let the fact stand or add a concrete consequence.
7. **Unsupported importance labels.** "This distinction matters." "This changes everything." Explain what changes for whom.
8. **Mechanical sentence stacks.** "You plan. You build. You scale." Vary the structure according to what the sentences say.
9. **Forced triads.** "Faster, smarter, better." Keep three real items; remove decorative lists that repeat the same idea.
10. **Clusters of stock vocabulary.** "Delve," "tapestry," "realm," "pivotal," and "ever-evolving landscape." Choose the concrete word. Keep precise technical uses.
11. **Generic scene-setting.** "In today's fast-paced digital world." "As AI continues to reshape the landscape." Start with the subject.
12. **Fake-profound metaphors.** "Trust is the currency of tomorrow." Replace the aphorism with the specific claim it is supposed to express.
13. **Dashes as the default connector.** Repeated interruptions and punchlines joined by em dashes. Use punctuation that explains the relationship. One dash does not establish AI authorship.
14. **Transformation and sales language.** "Unlock your potential." "Supercharge your workflow." Name the action, mechanism, or supported outcome.
15. **Superficial analysis clauses.** "...highlighting its significance." "...underscoring the importance of innovation." Keep only supported consequences or explanations.
16. **Vague borrowed authority.** "Experts agree." "Studies show." Name the source and what it found; do not invent consensus.
17. **Dramatic colon reveals.** "The real advantage: simplicity." State the observation in a normal sentence. Keep colons for actual lists and labels.
18. **Manufactured candor.** "Honestly?" "Let's be real." "I want you to sit with this." Keep a real admission; remove a staged entrance before a routine claim.
19. **Decorative formatting.** Emojis on every heading, scattered bold, or headings above tiny sections. Format to help navigation rather than manufacture emphasis.
20. **Recaps and inspirational endings that add nothing.** A final summary of everything just said or a generic promise about the future. End on the last useful point or next action.

These are editing instructions, not an automatic filter on every response. Apply the skill when writing or reviewing, and run the final check before returning the result.
